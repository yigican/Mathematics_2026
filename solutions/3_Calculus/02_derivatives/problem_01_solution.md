# Problem 1. Derivative of a Polynomial and the Slope of a Tangent Line

## Question

For

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4
$$

find $f'(x)$. Then compute $f'(0)$ and $f'(1)$, determine the signs of the obtained values, and explain what they say about the slope of the tangent line to the graph at these two points.

> **Why this problem:** it reinforces the power rule and connects the value of the derivative at a point with the geometric meaning of the tangent slope.

## Solution

The sum and constant-multiple rules allow each polynomial term to be differentiated separately. Using $(x^n)'=nx^{n-1}$,

$$
f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7.
$$

At the requested points, $f'(0)=7$ and $f'(1)=15-6+7=16$. Both are positive, so both tangent lines slope upward from left to right. The tangent at $x=1$ is steeper.

**Check:** Differentiating the constant gives zero; evaluating the individual derivative terms at $0$ and $1$ reproduces the two slopes.
