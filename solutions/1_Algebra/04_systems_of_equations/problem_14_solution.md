# Exercise 14. A Small Change in the Right-Hand Side

For both systems, the first two equations are

$$
x+y=2,\qquad 2x-y=1.
$$

Adding them eliminates $y$:

$$
3x=3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=1,\qquad y=1.
$$

**First system:** The third equation is $3x=3$. Substitution gives $3(1)=3$, so all equations hold. There is exactly one solution, $(1,1)$.

**Second system:** The third equation is $3x=3.1$. Substitution gives $3(1)=3\neq3.1$, so there is no solution.

**Elimination check:** Subtract the sum of the first two equations from the third. In the first system the result is $0=0$; in the second it is $0=0.1$, a contradiction.

**Explanation:** The first two lines have a unique intersection. The third condition agrees with it in the first system but excludes that point in the second. Even a small right-hand-side change can therefore change the system from consistent to inconsistent.
