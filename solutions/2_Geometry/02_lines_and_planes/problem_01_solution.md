# Exercise 1. Line Through Two Points

For $A=(1,2)$ and $B=(4,-1)$, a direction vector is

$$
d=B-A=(3,-3)=3(1,-1).
$$

Using the simpler direction $(1,-1)$, the parametric form is

$$
(x,y)=(1,2)+t(1,-1),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
$$

A normal vector must be perpendicular to $d$; choose $n=(1,1)$ because $n\cdot d=1-1=0$. The equation through $A$ is

$$
(x-1)+(y-2)=0,\qquad x+y-3=0.
$$

**Checks:** $A$ gives $1+2-3=0$ and $B$ gives $4-1-3=0$. In parametric form, $t=0$ gives $A$ and $t=3$ gives $B$.

**Explanation:** The direction runs along the line, whereas the normal vector is perpendicular to it.
