# Exercise 5. The Classical Limit Leading to the Number $e$

## Question

Compute

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n.
$$

Calculate several values for increasing $n$ and compare the observed behavior with the value of the limit. Explain why a few values do not constitute a proof.

> **Why this exercise:** connects a central limit with intuition based on concrete terms of a sequence.

## Solution

This is the defining classical sequence for Euler's number:

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+1/n)^n=e\approx2.718281828.
$$

For $n=1,2,5,10,100$, the terms are approximately $2$, $2.25$, $2.48832$, $2.59374$, $2.70481$. They increase toward $e$; the standard limit theorem (or a proof using logarithms and estimates) establishes the limiting value. No finite table proves convergence, because infinitely many later terms remain unexamined.
