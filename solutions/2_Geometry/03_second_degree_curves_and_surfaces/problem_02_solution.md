# Exercise 2. Circle by Completing the Square

## Question

Reduce

$$
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0
$$

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

> **Why this exercise:** practices completing the square as a transition from a general equation to the geometry of the object.

## Solution

Begin with

$$
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0.
$$

Group by variable and complete both squares:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2-6x)+(y^2+4y)&=3\\
(x-3)^2-9+(y+2)^2-4&=3\\
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2&=16.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is a circle with center $C=(3,-2)$ and radius $r=4$.

Two simple points are $P=(7,-2)$ and $Q=(3,2)$.

**Checks in canonical form:**

$$
(7-3)^2+(-2+2)^2=16,\qquad
(3-3)^2+(2+2)^2=16.
$$

**Checks in original form:**

$$
49+4-42-8-3=0,\qquad
9+4-18+8-3=0.
$$

Both descriptions give the same circle.
