# Exercise 7. Classifying a Second-Degree Equation

## Question

Determine the type of object described by:

1. $4x^2+9y^2=36$,
2. $x^2-y^2=1$,
3. $x^2-4y=0$,
4. $x^2+y^2+1=0$.

In the last case, explain why there are no real points.

> **Why this exercise:** develops quick recognition of the curve type and checking whether real solutions exist.

## Solution

**1.** $4x^2+9y^2=36$. Divide by $36$:

$$
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
$$

This is an **ellipse** centered at the origin, with semiaxes $3$ and $2$.

**2.** $x^2-y^2=1$ is a **hyperbola**, opening along the $x$-axis. The opposite signs of the squared terms identify it; $(\pm1,0)$ are its vertices.

**3.** $x^2-4y=0$ can be written $y=x^2/4$. This is an **upward-opening parabola** with vertex at the origin.

**4.** $x^2+y^2+1=0$ has **no real points**. For real $x,y$, $x^2+y^2\ge0$, so $x^2+y^2+1\ge1>0$.

**Checks:** The ellipse contains $(3,0)$, the hyperbola contains $(1,0)$, and the parabola contains $(2,1)$. None can be substituted into the fourth equation to give zero because its entire left side is strictly positive.
